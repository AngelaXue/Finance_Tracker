General — applies across pages

Quick start

From nothing to your first numbers
  1. Add your accounts on Net Worth → Add Account: name, institution, type, currency. Add a starting balance if the account already has money in it that you won't import.
  2. Download statements as CSV from each bank's website (look for Download / Export).
  3. Import them on the Import page: pick the account, add the files, Preview, Import.
  4. Review the yellow rows on Transactions — categories that were filled in automatically. Fix the Type of anything that's really a transfer.
  5. Repeat steps 2–4 once a month. Update investment values on Investments when you get a statement.

To look around with fake data first, stop the app and start it with python app.py --demo. Demo data lives in its own database; your real data is never touched.

Transaction Type vs. Category

What decides whether a row counts as spending, income, or a transfer
  • Type (transaction_type) decides what a row counts toward. Cash Flow, Spending, and Investments all read it.
  • Category is a free-text label for your own reference — filtering, searching, the Spending breakdown. Only one rule reads it: the Spending page's pass-through switch.

So changing a row's category alone never changes what it counts toward. Amounts are signed: negative = money out, positive = money in.

Which type to pick

SituationTypeExtra step
Ordinary income or spending income / expense—
A real purchase, but not your money (e.g. a gift someone else paid for on your card) expense Select it, click "Mark Not Mine" — see Not Mine
Between your own accounts: checking → savings, paying off your own card, ATM withdrawal into your Cash account internal_transfer (both sides)—
Between you and another person, but not income or spending: paying someone back, being paid back, money you're holding for someone personal_transfer Usually also an IOU — see Personal transfers
Moving money into an investment account investment_contribution Name the account in the note — see Investments

"Not Mine"

Real purchases that weren't your money

For a transaction that is a real purchase (or real income) worth categorizing, but wasn't your own money — e.g. something a relative bought for themselves on a card in your name, or money that landed in your account only to be handed on.

  • On Transactions, tick the row and click Mark Not Mine. Mark Mine undoes it. Not Mine rows show in italics with a colored bar; filter with Not Mine only.
  • Cash Flow: left out of income vs. spending.
  • Spending: still counted — "how much went to Dining on this card" is still a real number.
  • IOUs: a Not Mine purchase on an account someone else pays isn't counted as owed.

Different from personal_transfer, which is for money moving with no purchase at all. A row never needs both.

Personal transfers & pass-through

Money between you and other people

personal_transfer is for money moving between you and someone else that isn't your income or spending. It behaves exactly like internal_transfer — left out of Cash Flow, Spending and "needs review" — but stays its own label so you can tell it apart.

  • Repaying someone for something already counted (a friend covered dinner, you Venmo them back): the dinner was the spending; the repayment is a personal transfer. Logging it as expense would count the dinner twice.
  • Being paid back — same idea, money in.
  • Pass-through — money that arrives for someone else and leaves again. Tag both legs.

To also track who owes whom, mark the row on Transactions with the IOU… menu — see IOUs.

Splitting a shared bill

You paid an $80 internet bill, half of it a roommate's: change the row to $40 expense (your half) and add a second $40 row typed personal_transfer, marked IOU I paid with their name. When they pay you back, mark that row They paid. Their balance goes back to zero.

Pass-through that comes in as income

Work reimbursements (pay a work expense, get reimbursed, pay the bill) often arrive as ordinary expense/income. Give those the category Pass-Through; Spending hides them by default — see Spending.

Credit card payoffs

Keeping payoffs from counting as spending twice

Paying off your own card from your own checking account is internal_transfer on both sides. The purchases were the spending; the payoff just moves money.

  • The card's side is usually tagged automatically — wording like "Payment Thank You" matches a rule.
  • The checking side is worded differently by every bank and often missed, which counts your purchases a second time. Check that its Type is internal_transfer.

To fix many at once, this tags checking rows that exactly match an already-tagged card payment within a few days (dry run first; add --apply once the count looks right):

python -m src.maintenance retag-card-payoffs "My Checking"

Cash & digital wallets

Venmo, PayPal and cash on hand

Accounts of type cash or digital_wallet have no statement, so their balance is always the sum of their own transactions. Log:

  • Money coming in (a Venmo payment received, being paid in cash)
  • Money spent straight from it
  • Each half of a transfer to/from a bank — e.g. an ATM withdrawal is a negative row on checking and a positive row on Cash, both internal_transfer

Create one on Net Worth → Add Account (no starting balance — log what's in it now as its first transaction).

Currencies

Accounts in more than one currency
  • Each account has its own currency, and its balances are always shown in it. Net Worth has one table per currency, never blended.
  • Totals that must add different currencies — the one Net Worth figure, Cash Flow, Spending, IOUs — are converted to {{ BASE_CURRENCY }} using fixed rates in src/fx.py. They're edited by hand, never fetched live; update them now and then.
  • To report in a different currency, or to add one that's missing, edit src/fx.py (the README explains how).

Date ranges

"This Month", custom ranges, per-panel ranges
  • "This Month" runs from the 1st through today.
  • A transaction dated in the future won't show under "This Month" — switch to "All" or a custom range.
  • The Year and Month dropdowns on Transactions take over from the period selector when set.
  • Investments defaults to All. Each investment account has its own range row; one you haven't touched follows the top row.

Importing statements

Which files work, signs, duplicates, older history
  • Where files go: each account has its own folder inside incoming_statements/, named after the account. The Import page's upload puts files there for you. The folder says which account a file belongs to — file names don't matter.
  • Which banks: Bank of America and Chase have dedicated readers. Any other CSV with a date column and an amount column (or Debit / Credit columns) is read automatically.
  • Signs: money out must be negative. Some card exports list purchases as positive; for a card or loan, Auto flips a file when most amounts are positive. Preview shows the first rows — if they look wrong, pick As in file or Flip all signs.
  • A layout that isn't recognized (day-first dates, decimal commas, no obvious columns) can be described once in config/csv_formats.json — see the README.
  • After importing, files that went in cleanly are deleted (they're a privacy liability once the data is in). A file with a problem stays, with the reason shown.
  • Overlapping downloads are fine: a row matching one already there is kept and flagged pink for you to check — see Duplicate flagging.
  • Older history from a spreadsheet can come in once through the Import page's "Bring in older history" section, using the templates there.

Terminal commands

The same imports without the browser, and one-off fixes

Imports

  • python -m src.parsers.csv_importer --audit then --import — statements in incoming_statements/
  • python -m src.parsers.history_importer --audit then --import — files in import_history/

One-off fixes

Each runs as python -m src.maintenance <command>, is a dry run by default (prints only a count), and needs --apply to change anything.

  • create-account "Name" --institution X --account-type savings — same as Add Account
  • set-owner "Name" other --person "Mom" — an account someone else pays
  • retag-transfers "Account" --note-contains "..." — repayments logged as expenses → personal_transfer
  • retag-card-payoffs "Checking Account" — card payoffs counted twice
  • list-balance-dates — diagnose a jump in the Net Worth trend
By page — in sidebar order

Overview

The headline number, trend line, and summary cards

Net worth number & trend line

  • The headline number and trend leave out brokerage/retirement accounts, so market swings between your updates don't move them. The Net Worth page includes everything.
  • The trend has its own date range (All by default). Weekly points for spans up to about 13 months; monthly for longer ones.
  • Every account is evaluated on the same date at each point, so a transfer between two accounts never shows up as a spike.

If you see an unexplained jump anyway, this prints each account's recorded-balance dates (dates only):

python -m src.maintenance list-balance-dates --start YYYY-MM-DD --end YYYY-MM-DD

The rest of the page

  • Balances by currency — cash-type balances per currency, plus a combined {{ BASE_CURRENCY }} line.
  • Money allocation and the small Sankey are the same numbers as Cash Flow.
  • Spending by category leaves out Pass-Through rows, like Spending does by default.
  • Debts, Loans & IOUs — card and loan balances, plus everyone you have an IOU balance with. IOUs are shown for reference only; they don't change net worth.

Net Worth

Account balances, Add Account, Set Balance, CDs & bonds

The tables

  • One table per currency, grouped into Cash / Investments / Credit / Loans, each totalled in its own currency. Underneath, Net Worth (all accounts, in {{ BASE_CURRENCY }}) is the one converted number.
  • Credit cards and loans show as negative (red), since they're money owed.
  • 30-Day is the change since 30 days ago.

How each balance is worked out

  • Checking, savings, cards, loans — the last recorded balance (a starting balance, a Set Balance entry, or a balance from a history import), plus every transaction dated after it. Stays current as you import.
  • …with no recorded balance — the sum of all its transactions, as if it started at 0. These get a grey from transactions tag. If one doesn't match your bank, use Set Balance with what the bank shows for a date (today is easiest).
  • Cash and digital wallets — always the sum of their transactions.
  • Brokerage/retirement — your last current value from Investments, plus deposits since.

Adding an account

  • Add Account at the bottom: name, institution, type, currency, and who it belongs to.
  • Starting balance + date (optional): what it held on that date — for money that was there before the transactions you'll import. For a credit card or loan, enter what you owe as a positive number.
  • Someone else pays it (e.g. a card in your name a family member pays off): kept out of Net Worth entirely, and its spending counts as owed to that person on IOUs.
  • Each new account gets its own statements folder for importing. Typing an unknown account name on Transactions never creates one — on purpose, so a typo can't make a duplicate.

CDs & Bonds

  • A memo of each one's terms — type, principal, rate / yield, dates, note. Never added into Net Worth; the money is already counted through the account it's held in.
  • Add with the form under the table; edit any cell, then Save changes (or Discard). One invalid value saves nothing.
  • Ones held in an investment account also show — and can be edited — in that account's panel on Investments.
  • Current Balance is the linked account's balance (the whole account, if it holds other money too). The rate is never used to estimate interest.

Cash Flow

What counts, and how income and outputs balance
  • Inflow — every income row in the period (not Not Mine), converted to {{ BASE_CURRENCY }}, grouped by category.
  • Living Expenses — every expense row (not Not Mine), across all accounts, accounts someone else pays included.
  • Investing — every investment_contribution.
  • Paid by Others — manual IOU entries where someone paid for something of yours directly (no transaction in your accounts).
  • Cash Savings = inflow minus all of the above, when positive. When you spent more than came in, the gap shows as From Savings on the left instead — the two sides always balance.
  • Transfers between your own accounts and personal transfers never appear. Pass-through typed as expense/income is counted here (usually a wash, since it goes in and back out).
  • Hover a link for its amount and its share of income.

Spending

The pass-through switch, categories
  • Only expense rows count, grouped by Category, each with its share and the change from the same-length period before. Click a category to see its transactions.
  • Not Mine rows are still counted here.
  • Pass-through switch: rows whose Category is Pass-Through (any spelling — "pass through", "Passthrough") are hidden by default; flip Include pass-through to count them. This is the one place a Category changes the math.

Transactions

Editing, logging by hand, suggestions, duplicates, categorization

Editing and logging by hand

  • Click any cell to edit it, then Save changes (or Discard). Nothing is saved until you click Save.
  • + Add row adds a transaction by hand. If the page is filtered to one account, a new row starts on that account.
  • Dates are YYYY-MM-DD (2026-1-9 is fine too). Amounts accept commas.
  • The filtered total under the table sums what's shown; tick rows for a live selected total.

Suggestions as you type

  • Account and Category suggest matches; Tab fills the only one.
  • Description suggests ones you've used before. Picking one also fills that row's empty Category and Method with what you usually use for it — and on a new row, its usual Type.

Duplicate flagging

A row matching an existing one's account, date and amount is flagged pink — from an import, a new row, or an edit. It's never dropped: two real transactions can share every field (two bus rides, same day, same fare).

  • Right after saving, a pop-up asks: Continue (not a duplicate), Discard (delete it), or Cancel (decide later).
  • Later: tick it and Save changes to confirm it, or Delete selected. Editing the row also clears the flag.

Categorization & "needs review"

  1. Rules (CATEGORY_RULES in src/categorize.py) — fixed patterns, e.g. "transfer" → internal_transfer.
  2. Your bank's own category, where the CSV has one (mapped to these categories).
  3. A local classifier trained only on your own categorized history, on this computer. It only guesses when reasonably confident; otherwise the category stays blank.
  • Anything filled in automatically is yellow ("needs review") until you edit or confirm it (tick + Save).
  • Each correction teaches the classifier — suggestions get better the more you categorize. Suggest categories re-runs it on everything uncategorized.

Mark buttons and the IOU column

  • Mark Not Mine / Mark Mine — see Not Mine.
  • The IOU… menu (with the Person box) and the IOU column — see IOUs.

Import

Upload, preview, import
  1. Add files: pick the account, choose the CSVs, Add files. (Or drop them in that account's folder yourself.)
  2. Preview: reads every waiting file and shows what would be added — row counts, dates, possible duplicates, and the first rows of each so you can check the signs. Nothing is saved.
  3. Import: adds the rows, suggests categories, and deletes each file that went in cleanly.

Wrong file? Remove deletes it without importing. More detail: Importing statements.

Investments

Principal, deposits, snapshots, All entries

Answers "how much do I have, and how much of it is growth" for each brokerage/retirement account. No holdings, no live prices — numbers come from your statements, typed in now and then.

The three numbers

  • Principal — money you put in: deposits picked up from Transactions plus any Principal added you type in.
  • Current value — your last typed-in value, plus deposits since (at face value) until the next one.
  • Growth = value − principal; Growth % is growth as a share of principal.

Deposits log themselves

  1. On Transactions, give the transfer from your bank Type investment_contribution.
  2. Name the account in its note — its full name or just its first word, any capitals (for accounts named "Roth IRA" and "Brokerage": roth, brokerage). If the note doesn't say, the description is checked too ("Transfer to Roth IRA").

Tip: give investment accounts distinctive first words, so a note is enough.

"Investment transactions that need a look"

Appears when a deposit names no account (or several), or a row has an Investment category but a different Type. Fix the note on Transactions (the dropdown fills itself in when you come back), or pick from Went to — including Not an investment. Then Save changes.

Add a Snapshot

  • Current value — what the statement says the account is worth that day (market moves included). The same account + date again replaces it.
  • Principal added — new money that isn't on Transactions (a transfer logged in both places would count twice; you'll be asked if one looks like that).

Charts and panels

  • An Overview panel, then one folding panel per account, each with its own date range.
  • The timeline is to scale; Principal steps up on each deposit; the light-amber band is money held in CDs & bonds (from each one's opened date to maturity).
  • All entries (bottom) lists everything behind the numbers; values and principal entries edit in place.

IOUs

Who owes whom — family, roommates, friends

One running balance per person: what you owe them. Positive (red) = you owe them; negative (green) = they owe you. It never changes net worth — it's a ledger beside it.

Two directions

  • They paid — money or value from them to you: they paid for something of yours, lent you money, or paid you back. Adds to what you owe them.
  • I paid — from you to them: you paid them back, lent them money, or paid for something of theirs. Reduces it (below zero means they owe you).

Where entries come from

  • Automatically: spending on an account set to Someone else pays it counts as They paid for that person (unless marked Not Mine); a refund on it counts the other way.
  • Marked by you: on Transactions, tick rows, type the person in the Person box, and pick from the IOU… menu. Not an IOU opts a row out; Reset to automatic undoes a choice. Nothing else is guessed — a transfer to a friend could be a loan, a repayment or a gift.
  • Manual entries on the IOUs page, for things with no transaction (cash, or they paid a bill directly). Don't add one for something that is a transaction — mark the transaction instead.

The IOU column on Transactions

  • Shows each row's direction and person. Outlined = set by you; plain = automatic. Hover for the reason.
  • The IOUs filter lists only They paid, only I paid, or both.

Starting balance, and Settled / Pending marks

  • Starting balance — "as of this date, the balance was exactly this much" (negative if they owed you). Replaces everything for that person on or before it, so old history doesn't need entering.
  • Mark Settled / Pending are reminders only — they never change a balance. What changes it is the repayment itself, marked on Transactions.
